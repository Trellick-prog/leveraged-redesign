How to spot a scam

Scams cost Australian consumers, businesses and the economy hundreds of millions of dollars each year and cause serious emotional harm to victims and their families. In 2021, combined scam losses reported to Scamwatch, banks and other government agencies was $1.8 billion. One third of victims do not report scams, so actual losses were well over $2 billion.

Warning signs

  • A threat if you don’t pay immediately.
  • An unexpected request that can’t wait.
  • A link to update your personal details.
  • An immediate payment so you don’t miss out.
  • An attachment or link you weren’t expecting.

Remember

  • Stop and check.
  • Protect your personal information.
  • Be careful with payments.
  • Verify before you buy.
  • Research any opportunity that is too good to be true.

Protect yourself and your investments

Protecting yourself, your family and your investments from scams and identity theft can save you a lot of inconvenience, disruption and money. These tips can help.

Phone

  • Criminals use phones as the number one delivery method for scams.
  • Never allow anyone to access your computer or device remotely.
  • Never provide your Leveraged Online access password to anyone.
  • Do not respond to unsolicited calls, SMS or email asking you for personal information or financial details.
  • Delete spam emails and do not open SMS or email attachments or click on links in SMS or emails from strangers.
  • Be aware of email, internet and telephone hoaxes and scams – if something appears too good to be true, it almost certainly is.
  • For more information about protecting yourself from scams, refer to the ACCC Scamwatch website.

Online

  • Don’t open suspicious texts, pop-up windows or click on links or attachments in unsolicited emails – delete them.
  • Don’t enter or provide your card number to unsolicited sites or callers.
  • Don’t respond to phone calls about your computer asking for remote access – hang up.
  • Keep your personal details secure.
  • Choose your passwords carefully.
  • Review your privacy and security settings on social media.
  • Beware of any requests for your personal details or money.
  • If you think you have been a victim of a scam, let us know as soon as possible by calling us.

General

  • Keep your computer up to date by installing the latest security software and ‘patches’ for your operating system.
  • Ensure you have anti-virus/spyware software installed, along with a firewall – and keep them up to date.
  • Make sure you have a current backup of your important personal information and files.
  • Delete spam emails and do not open email attachments or click on links in emails from strangers.
  • Do not respond to an unsolicited email asking you for personal information or financial details.
  • If possible, avoid using shared computers, such as those at internet cafes, hotels and airports.
  • For more on staying secure online, visit the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC).

Cheque fraud

Not many people use cheques these days, but cheque fraud is still prevalent. To help reduce it:

  • Keep your chequebook in a safe and secure place.
  • Never pre-sign cheques.
  • When mailing cheques, cross the cheque ‘Not Negotiable’ and send it in a plain envelope without a window.
  • Use a pen or felt tip pen to write on your cheques, never pencil.
  • Bank cheques should not be considered as good as cash.
  • Don’t accept cheques in excess of the agreed price where you are asked to transfer or deposit the balance to a specific account.

Investment scams

Investment scams are often disguised as an offer to purchase cryptocurrency (like Bitcoin), business ventures, superannuation schemes, managed funds, or shares or property.

Scammers create ‘opportunities’ with professional brochures, websites and advertisements. These mask fraudulent activities and trick people into taking up the offer.

How to protect yourself

  • Before putting money into an investment, do some of your own research. You can check whether a company has an Australian Financial Services Licence at moneysmart.gov.au.
  • Watch out for offers promoting easy and early access to your superannuation.
  • Don’t be pressured into making an investment decision, especially if the opportunity came via an unsolicited email or phone call. Always seek advice from a reputable financial adviser or raise any concerns with our team.

Received a suspicious SMS?

If you have received a suspicious SMS claiming to be from us, forward it to 0429 557 997 for investigation. Take care not to click on the link while doing so. Alternatively, take a screenshot of the message and email it to phishing@bendigoadelaide.com.au.

Apple device

  1. Tap and hold the SMS message until the options ‘Copy/More’ appear.
  2. Select ‘More’.
  3. Tap the arrow in the bottom right corner to ‘Share/Forward’.
  4. Type 0429 557 997 as the recipient and press ‘Send’.
  5. You can then delete the original and the forwarded message.

Android device

  1. Tap and hold the SMS message until the three-dot menu icon appears in the top right corner.
  2. Tap the three-dot menu icon.
  3. Select ‘Forward’.
  4. Type 0429 557 997 as the recipient and press ‘Send’.
  5. You can then delete the original and the forwarded message.

Protecting your children

Increasingly, children are being targeted online and can be vulnerable to aggressive behaviour and criminal activity. They use the internet regularly and interact via social media, but are often too inexperienced to recognise when they are being deceived. Education is the best way to help protect them.

Resources for parents and children

  • The Australian Government’s eSafety Kids website and the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC)
  • The eSmart Schools website
  • The ThinkUKnow website for parents, carers, teachers and young people 11–17 years old
