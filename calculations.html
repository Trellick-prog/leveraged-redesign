Key terms and how they're calculated

These figures appear in your online account and statements, and update through the trading day as prices move.

TermWhat it meansHow it's calculated
Market valueWhat all the investments in your loan portfolio are worth today.Sum of (units × current price)
Lending ratioThe percentage of an investment's value we'll lend against. Each investment has its own, set on the acceptable investments list.Set per investment, e.g. 75%, 70%, 50%
Lending valueThe most you can borrow against your portfolio as it stands.Sum of (market value × lending ratio)
Loan balanceWhat you currently owe.Previous balance + drawdowns and purchases + interest and fees − repayments and sale proceeds
Credit limitThe maximum we've approved you to borrow, whatever your lending value.Set when your application is approved
Available fundsHow much more you can borrow right now.Lower of lending value or credit limit − loan balance
GearingHow much of your portfolio is funded by the loan. Not the same as the lending ratio.Loan balance ÷ market value × 100
BufferExtra room above your lending value before a margin call is made.Market value × buffer % (usually 10%)

Worked examples

A portfolio of two shares worth $50,000 each, with a $40,000 loan and a $100,000 credit limit.

1. Market value

Add up what each holding is worth.

$50,000 + $50,000
= $100,000

2. Lending value

Share A has a 75% lending ratio, share B 50%.

($50,000 × 75%) + ($50,000 × 50%)
= $37,500 + $25,000
= $62,500

3. Available funds

Lending value ($62,500) is lower than the credit limit ($100,000), so it sets the cap. Subtract your $40,000 loan balance (what you’ve already borrowed) to get what’s left to draw.

$62,500 lending value − $40,000 loan balance
= $22,500
What if the credit limit were $50,000?It would be lower than the lending value, so it sets the cap instead. Available funds would be $50,000 credit limit − $40,000 loan balance = $10,000.

4. Gearing

How much of the portfolio the loan funds.

$40,000 ÷ $100,000 × 100
= 40%

When a margin call happens

A margin call is made when your loan balance goes above your lending value plus the buffer. This usually happens because the market value of your investments falls, which lowers your lending value.

Margin call if: loan balance > lending value + buffer
Same portfolioTodayAfter a 45% fall
Market value$100,000$55,000
Lending value$62,500$34,375
Buffer (10% of market value)$10,000$5,500
Lending value + buffer$72,500$39,875

If you get a margin call, you'll usually need to act within 24 hours by repaying some of the loan, adding cash or acceptable investments, or selling investments. If you don't, we can sell investments for you. Between your lending value and the buffer limit, you can't draw more funds, but no call is made.

Concentration limits

To encourage a spread of investments, some investments get a higher lending ratio while they make up no more than a set share of your portfolio, called the concentration limit. Any amount above that limit gets a lower ratio. Only the excess is affected, not the whole holding.

Each investment's concentration limit, and the lending ratios either side of it, are on the acceptable investments list. Check the latest list, as these can change.

Example: $60,000 of one share in a $100,000 portfolio

Say this share has a 40% concentration limit, a 60% lending ratio up to the limit and 40% above it. The limit is 40% of $100,000 = $40,000.

Part of the holdingValueLending ratioLending value
Up to the limit$40,00060%$24,000
Above the limit$20,00040%$8,000
Whole holding$60,000–$32,000

Without the limit, the full $60,000 at 60% would give $36,000. Keeping each holding under its limit gets you the most borrowing power.

Transfer investments out↓ by their value↓ by value × lending ratioNo change↓ by value × lending ratio

Changes to available funds assume your lending value, not your credit limit, is the cap. If the credit limit is the cap, transfers don't change available funds.
