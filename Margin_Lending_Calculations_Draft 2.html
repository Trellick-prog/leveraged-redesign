Understanding Your Margin Loan Calculations

A comprehensive investor guide explaining core metrics, lending ratios, two-tier portfolio concentration rules, and everyday transaction impacts.

1 Core Terms and How They Are Calculated

Below are the primary figures displayed across your margin loan dashboard and monthly statements, outlining their operational definitions and exact calculation logic:

Term What It Means How It Is Calculated
Market Value (MV) Total current dollar value of all holdings in your margin loan portfolio. Sum of (Quantity × Current Unit Price)
Lending Ratio / LVR (%) The approved borrowing percentage assigned to each security by the lender. Set per security (e.g., 75%, 70%, 50%, or 40%)
Lending Value (LV) Maximum dollar amount you can borrow against your approved portfolio. Sum of (Market Value × Applicable LVR %)
Loan Balance Current total borrowed amount, including accrued interest and fees. Opening Balance + Buys/Drawdowns - Repayments/Sells
Credit Limit The maximum borrowing cap formally approved for your facility. Approved limit established during credit assessment
Available Funds Unutilised borrowing capacity available for cash drawdown or trading. Lower of (Lending Value OR Credit Limit) - Loan Balance
Buffer Safety cushion above your Lending Value before a Margin Call is triggered. Total Market Value × Buffer % (typically 10%)
Current LVR (%) Actual gearing ratio measuring total loan balance against portfolio value. (Loan Balance ÷ Total Market Value) × 100

2 Practical Calculation Rules

Your account metrics update automatically throughout the trading day as asset prices fluctuate:

A. Total Market Value

Sum the current market values of all assets held:

$50k (Stock A) + $50k (Stock B)
= $100,000 Market Value

B. Total Lending Value

Multiply each holding by its assigned LVR, then add together:

($50k × 75%) + ($50k × 50%)
= $37,500 + $25,000 = $62,500

C. Available Funds

Governed by the lesser of Lending Value or Credit Limit:

Lending Value $62,500 - Loan $40,000
= $22,500 Available Funds

3 Concentration Limits: Encouraging a Diversified Portfolio

To encourage diversification and mitigate single-stock exposure risk, lenders provide higher borrowing ratios when individual holdings remain below specified portfolio weightings.

Acceptable Investment List (AIL) Reference: Each security has its own specific Concentration Limit, Diversified LVR, and Standard LVR published on the Acceptable Investment List. Always refer to the live AIL to verify asset limits.

The Two-Tier Split Principle: When a stock exceeds its concentration limit, the position is tiered rather than penalised in full:

  • Up to Concentration Limit: Receives the higher Diversified LVR.
  • Above Concentration Limit (the excess): Receives the lower Standard LVR.

Worked Example: Macquarie Group (MQG)

Portion of Holding Dollar Value Applicable LVR Lending Value
Up to 50% Limit $50,000 50% Diversified LVR $25,000
Excess over 50% (10%) $10,000 40% Standard LVR $4,000
Total MQG Position (60%) $60,000 — $29,000
Rule of Thumb: Total MQG Lending Value = (50% gets 50%) + (10% gets 40%) = $29,000. Maintaining individual holdings below their AIL concentration thresholds maximises total borrowing capacity.
